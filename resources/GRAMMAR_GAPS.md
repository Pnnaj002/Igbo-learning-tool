## 1. Orthography
Dotted vowels: ị ọ ụ are separate letters (not accents). Digraphs: ch, gb, gh, gw, kp, kw, nw, ny, sh. Also ṅ.
Drill: read a BBC Igbo paragraph aloud daily; type words with dots until your keyboard setup is fluent.

## 2. Tone (three levels: high, low, downstep)
Tone changes meaning. Classic example: **ákwá** (cry), **àkwà** (bed), **ákwà** (cloth), **àkwá** (egg).
Written Igbo usually omits tone marks, so you rely on your ear: record yourself saying the four and ask a tutor to check.
Drill: 10 minimal pairs per week; shadow audio, matching pitch, not just words.

## 3. Verbs: tense and aspect (the biggest payoff)
Igbo verbs have no conjugation by person; the pattern comes from the helper/suffix. Pattern with **eri** (eat):
| Meaning | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present/habitual (progressive) | **na-** + verb (often `ana m`) | Ana m eri nri. (I'm eating.) |
| Future | **ga-** + verb | Aga m eri nri. (I'll eat.) |
| Simple past | verb + **-rọ/-rụ** or reduplicated stem | Eriri m nri. (I ate.) |
| Perfect ("have done") | verb + **-la/-le** | Eriela m nri. (I have eaten.) |
| Negative | verb + **-ghị** | Erighị m nri. (I don't/didn't eat.) |
| Command | bare root | Rie nri! (Eat!) |
Drill: take 10 common verbs (ri, ga, bịa, mee, were, ṅụọ, gụọ, ṅụ, kwuo, hụ) and say each in all six patterns, aloud, in under 5 seconds each.

## 4. Vowel harmony
Vowels in a word come from one of two sets: **i, u, e, o** vs. **ị, ụ, a, ọ**; prefixes and suffixes match the root.
Example: Ị gara (you went; a-set root) vs. I jere (you went; e-set root). Verify these with a tutor.
Drill: when you notice a form you say differently from the textbook, ask which vowel set the root belongs to.

## 5. Pronouns and subject marking
Subject pronouns: m / ị~i / ọ~o~ya / anyị / unu / ha. Possessives: **nke m** (mine), **nke gị**, **nke ya**; plural people: **ndị**, children **ụmụ**. Igbo has no grammatical gender and nouns do not take a plural suffix.

## 6. Question words
Gịnị (what), Onye (who), Ebee (where), Kedu (how/which), Ole (how many), Olee mgbe (when), Gịnị mere (why).
Drill: ask 20 questions about your day aloud each week.

## 7. Relative clauses, connectors, conditionals
Connectors: na (and/that), mana (but), ma (or), maka (because/for), ọ bụrụ na (if). Practice joining two short sentences into one with each.
Heritage-speaker trap: strings of short sentences. Force yourself to use connectors.

## 8. Numbers, time, money
Count to 100 fast, then 1000s. Tell time, dates, prices. Common weak spot for heritage speakers (families often mix in English numbers).

## 9. Register: elders, respect, proverbs (ilu)
Greetings by time of day, ways to address elders, polite requests. Collect 1 new proverb a week, say it aloud, explain it in your own words. Ask your parents for the stories behind them.
