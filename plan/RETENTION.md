# Protecting What You Already Know

A risk with a formal plan: you start "correcting" things you used to say naturally, or drop knowledge you
never wrote down. These tools prevent that.

## 1. Week 1 baseline recordings (never delete)
Record these on Day 1-3, before studying anything:
1. 3 minutes talking freely about your family and your day, in Igbo, as naturally as you can.
2. Listen to a native clip (BBC Igbo or a parent on a voice note) and **summarize it aloud** (in English or Igbo).
3. Describe 10 household objects, 10 foods, 10 family relationships out loud. Mark which you hesitated on.
Re-do all three in Wk 12 and Wk 24. Compare: speed, hesitation, vocabulary range, errors.

## 2. The Vault (things you already know)
- Make a separate SRS deck (Anki or similar) called **Vault**. Add phrases, proverbs, family expressions and
  words you already know cold, and the ones you know but are rusty on.
- Review the Vault at low intensity: 10 random cards, 3 times per week. If you hesitate, flag the card and move it
  to your active deck.
- Aim for **audio on every card**: record a parent or yourself so the pronunciation of *your* dialect is preserved.

## 3. Dialect log (`tracking/MY_DIALECT_NOTES.md`)
Whenever you learn a Standard Igbo word/form that differs from your family's, log both. Format:
`Meaning | Family form | Standard (Izugbe) form | Notes`.
This lets you code-switch on purpose, and nothing is lost.

## 4. Monthly "old knowledge" audit
Once a month, ask a parent or relative for a 20-minute conversation, only in Igbo, about everyday things.
Note: (a) what you understood without effort (should stay at ~100%), (b) any words you missed.
If comprehension drops, add 10 minutes of listening per day.

## 5. Spaced review calendar for grammar rules
Each time you finish a grammar topic, re-test it at +1 day, +1 week, +1 month, +3 months
(produce 5 sentences aloud with no notes). Tracked in `tracking/PROGRESS.md`.

## 6. Listening floor
Keep at least 2 hours/week of passive listening (parents' calls, Igbo radio, podcasts) so comprehension never regresses.
